# IGP 2018: first verification of coare-seaice

*16 Nov 2026, coare-seaice release 0.4.0 (branch `release/0.4.0`). Script:
`scripts/igp_verify_ship_aircraft.py`. Data: the IGP Alliance 10-min ship record
(`IGP_Alliance_Surface_Met_QC3_10min.nc`, CEDA) and the IGP aircraft long-run flux
database (`IGP_flights_database_obs_lr.txt`). The full method is in the script's
docstring and is written out with every run. These are first results, not paper
results.*

## A. Ship fluxes recomputed against the stored COARE 3.0a fluxes

The ship file holds bulk fluxes made with COARE 3.0a in 2020. coare-seaice (open
water, COARE 3.5 through `pycoare`) was run on the same measured inputs, for the 3467
10-min windows at sea that have every input measured. Heights: wind 40 m (LIDAR, from
the file); temperature and humidity about 8 m above sea level (from the ship team; not
in the file).

| | new minus stored: median | mean | RMSE | r |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stress (N m⁻²) | −0.0005 | +0.0028 | 0.013 | 1.00 |
| sensible heat (W m⁻²) | +2.7 | +2.6 | 2.9 | 1.00 |
| latent heat (W m⁻²) | +1.5 | +1.5 | 1.8 | 1.00 |

- **The open-water path and the input handling are right.** The values correlate at
  1.00 and differ by a few W m⁻².
- **The heat-flux offset depends on the temperature and humidity height.** Scanning that
  height against the stored fluxes gives these median differences (sensible, latent):

  | zt = zq | 5 m | 8 m | 10 m | 15 m | 20 m | 25 m | 30 m |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | W m⁻² | +4.0, +2.7 | +2.7, +1.5 | +1.8, +0.9 | +0.7, +0.2 | −0.2, −0.0 | −1.0, −0.3 | −1.8, −0.5 |

  The stored fluxes are matched almost exactly at 20 m. Either the 2020 processing used
  a height near 20 m, or the COARE 3.0a to 3.5 changes happen to offset a lower height.
  This cannot be separated here. **Check the height used in the 2020 processing.** If it
  was 8 m, the 2.7 and 1.5 W m⁻² offsets are the 3.0a to 3.5 change. Stress is higher at
  high wind, from the new Charnock dependence.
- The file labels SST as K and relative humidity as "1". The values are °C and per
  cent.

## B. Ship bulk fluxes against aircraft eddy covariance near the ship

Only aircraft runs at or below 60 m are used. Each is paired with the ship window within
10 min. Distance from the ship is relaxed in steps from 10 to 200 km to test
sensitivity. 16 low runs qualify in total. Within 50 km, there are 10, all from flight
294 (1 March), 0.5–42 km from the ship, at 44–52 m, with ice fraction 0–0.5. Six runs
from flight 305 (18 March) are 176–195 km away, at 15–17 m, over open water. The ship
bulk fluxes are blended with each run's ice fraction.

Bulk minus aircraft (median), cumulative distance:

| within | stress (N m⁻²) | sensible (W m⁻²) | latent (W m⁻²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 km | −0.017 (n=3) | +27 (3) | +39 (3) |
| 25 km | −0.016 (4) | +31 (4) | +39 (4) |
| 50 km | −0.008 (10) | +34 (9) | +39 (4) |
| 200 km | +0.012 (14) | +46 (15) | +49 (10) |

- **Within 50 km, distance makes little difference.** The heat-flux bias is +27 to
  +34 W m⁻² from 10 to 50 km, and stress is within ±0.02 N m⁻².
- **The bias near the ship comes from the bulk method, not the distance.** Bulk fluxes
  computed from the aircraft's own measurements on the same flight-294 runs show the same
  bias: sensible +45, latent +38 W m⁻². The ship and aircraft bulk estimates agree within
  7 W m⁻² (median). The aircraft fluxes on this flight are low compared with any bulk
  estimate (sensible 1–47 W m⁻², latent 3–10 W m⁻²).
- **At 180 km the ship was in different air.** On flight 305, the ship bulk sensible heat
  is about 115 W m⁻², against about 40 from the aircraft's own inputs and 18–36 measured.
  Those runs are not a test of the bulk method.
- **This is a loose test.** There are few runs, mostly from one flight, the surfaces
  differ, and the aircraft fluxes are at flight level.
- Earlier runs at 60–150 m are excluded. They included flight 295, where the aircraft
  latent heat flux looked suspect: −21 to +38 W m⁻² in a strong cold-air outbreak with
  sensible heat 125–215 W m⁻². That should still be checked.

## C. Aircraft-driven bulk fluxes against aircraft eddy covariance, by ice fraction

The bulk algorithm was driven with the aircraft's own mean measurements, for every
quality-controlled run at or below 60 m over the sea (up to 146 runs). Bias is bulk
minus aircraft (median). There are two configurations:
- the reference: two tiles, ice at min(T_air, −1.8 °C), z0_ice = 5 × 10⁻⁴ m;
- one aggregate surface temperature from the aircraft radiometer.

| ice fraction | stress, reference (N m⁻²) | sensible, reference (W m⁻²) | sensible, one temperature (W m⁻²) | latent, reference (W m⁻²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | +0.002 | +8 | +6 | +20 |
| 0–0.25 | −0.002 | +26 | +11 | +23 |
| 0.25–0.5 | −0.089 | +41 | +29 | +17 |
| 0.5–0.75 | −0.041 | +25 | +10 | +10 |
| 0.75–1 | −0.055 | +11 | +3 | +4 |
| 1 | −0.063 | −3 | +5 | −0 |
| all (RMSE) | 0.100 | 30.0 | 21.7 | 36.2 |

These reproduce the design-phase findings:

- **MIZ heat fluxes are too high with two tiles.** The sensible heat bias is +25 to
  +41 W m⁻² at ice fractions 0.25–0.75. The bias falls when one radiometer
  temperature is used: overall RMSE drops from 30 to 22 W m⁻². This points to the
  non-thick-ice surface (thin ice, the cold MIZ surface) not being at the SST. That is
  the motivation for the thin-ice tile (T10).
- **Open-sea scalar fluxes are high.** The latent heat bias at zero ice is +20 W m⁻²,
  consistent with the aircraft's open-sea scalar coefficients being below COARE's
  (Elvidge et al. 2021).
- **Stress over high ice fractions is too low** (−0.06 N m⁻²). The default
  z0_ice = 5 × 10⁻⁴ m is too smooth for this ice, as found before (IGP drag about
  3.4 × 10⁻³). The named regional roughness `"iceland_sea"` is the obvious next test.
- **Pack ice (ice fraction 1) is well reproduced** for the heat fluxes: medians within
  3 W m⁻².

## Caveats

- Aircraft fluxes are at flight level (15–60 m in part C), with no flux-divergence
  correction.
- One radiometer temperature represents a 9 km run.
- Database quality flags (`z0_flag`, `z0T_flag`, `z0q_flag` ≤ 1) were used. They are
  roughness-fit flags and do not catch every bad flux (see flight 295 above).
- Nothing was tuned to these data.
